## 1. Purpose

To protect the privacy and confidentiality of every member's records, protected health
information (PHI), and personally identifiable information (PII) in compliance with HIPAA,
A.R.S. § 36-568.01 and § 36-568.02, A.A.C. R6-6-808.G, R6-6-809.N, R6-6-810.A & B, R6-6-906.B,
and DDD Medical Policy Manual Chapter 550 (Member Records and Confidentiality).

## 2. Policy

- FFI limits access to member records and PHI/PII to staff with a legitimate, service-related
  need to know.
- No member information is released to any outside party without a signed, informed consent for
  release of information from the member or responsible person, except where disclosure is
  required by law (for example, mandatory reporting, Division requests, or court order).
- Members' confidential information is safeguarded every day, in every setting — in the office, in
  members' homes, at worksites, in vehicles, and online.
- Confidentiality breaches are incidents. They are reported verbally and in writing under FFI-02
  (Incident Reporting), consistent with DES/DDD incident reporting procedure.

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Safeguards and storage

- Physical records are stored in locked cabinets at the FFI office; keys are limited to the
  Executive Director, Program Manager, and Quality & Compliance Coordinator.
- Electronic records are stored in access-controlled, encrypted systems with unique logins per
  staff member; access rights follow each staff member's role and are revoked on separation.
- Records in daily use (for example, service notes at a member's home or worksite) are kept out of
  view of visitors and returned to secure storage the same day.
- Daily safeguards include: screens locked when unattended, no member documents left in vehicles,
  no discussion of member information in public areas, and secure (encrypted) email for any
  transmission of PHI.

### 3.2 Sharing member information

- Before sharing any member information, staff verify and document: who is asking, what specific
  information is requested, the purpose, and whether a current signed release covers the request.
- Requests are logged by the Quality & Compliance Coordinator in the member's record.
- The Division, and entities authorized by law, may access records upon request.

### 3.3 Informed consent and photography

- A signed Release of Information form is obtained before any disclosure of confidential
  information and renewed at least annually or when the scope changes.
- A separate, specific Photography/Media Consent is required before any photo, video, or audio of
  a member is taken, used, or displayed. Consent may be limited or withdrawn at any time.

### 3.4 Social media, marketing, and advertising

- Staff must not post, share, or reference any member (name, image, identifiable detail, or
  service relationship) on any social networking site, personal or agency, without a current
  Photography/Media Consent and Executive Director approval.
- FFI marketing and advertising materials never include member PHI or identifiable member
  information without documented consent.

### 3.5 Portable equipment

- Only FFI-approved devices (agency-issued or approved personal smartphones, laptops, and
  encrypted flash drives) may hold or access member information.
- Devices must be password-protected and encrypted; member information may not be stored on
  personal cloud accounts. Lost or stolen devices are reported immediately to the Executive
  Director and treated as a potential breach.

### 3.6 Records destruction

- Records past their retention period (see FFI-05) are destroyed by cross-cut shredding (paper) or
  certified secure deletion (electronic). Destruction is logged with date, records description, and
  the staff member responsible.

## 4. Training requirements

- HIPAA/Confidentiality training is completed at hire (before working with members) and annually
  thereafter, delivered in person or online by the Training Coordinator, and includes review of
  A.R.S. § 36-568.01.
- Training covers safeguards, consent procedures, photography/social media rules, portable
  equipment, and breach reporting. Comprehension is verified by a written or electronic quiz;
  results are kept in the personnel file.

## 5. Documentation and recordkeeping

Signed confidentiality agreements, consents, release logs, breach reports, and destruction logs
are maintained per FFI-05 (minimum seven years).

## 6. Monitoring and oversight

The Quality & Compliance Coordinator audits a sample of member files and staff practices
quarterly (storage, consents on file, release logs) and reviews findings with the Executive
Director. This policy is reviewed with all staff and members at least annually and when revised.

## 7. Compliance and corrective action

Violations result in corrective action proportionate to the breach — from retraining, up to
termination and referral to authorities where required. Any breach affecting a member is reported
to the Division per FFI-02, with an internal review to correct the system weakness that allowed it.
