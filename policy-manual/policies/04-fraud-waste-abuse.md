## 1. Purpose

To prevent, detect, and report fraud, waste, and abuse (FWA) in Division-funded services,
consistent with DDD Provider Policy Manual Chapters 20 (Fraud, Waste and Abuse) and 21 (False
Claims Act).

## 2. Prevention and detection

- FFI bills only for services actually delivered, by qualified staff, to authorized members, in
  authorized amounts, supported by contemporaneous documentation.
- Internal controls include: separation of duties between service documentation and billing;
  supervisor verification of timesheets against service logs (FFI-05); monthly reconciliation of
  claims against authorizations; monthly SAM and OIG/LEIE exclusion checks for all staff; and
  random documentation audits by the Quality & Compliance Coordinator.

## 3. Code of Conduct and misconduct

FFI maintains a well-publicized Code of Conduct, distributed at hire and posted at the office,
requiring honesty in documentation and billing and prohibiting falsification of any record.

- **Reporting suspicious actions:** any suspicious action observed by or reported to staff — by a
  member, representative, or another staff member — must be reported to the Executive Director or
  Quality & Compliance Coordinator immediately. Reports may be made anonymously.
- **Referral and reporting:** confirmed or reasonably suspected FWA is reported to the Division
  (DDD Customer Service / Office of Inspector General referral channels) and, where applicable,
  AHCCCS OIG. FFI cooperates fully with all investigations.
- **Whistleblower protections:** FFI prohibits retaliation of any kind against anyone who reports
  suspected FWA in good faith, consistent with federal and state whistleblower protections
  (including FCA qui tam provisions, 31 U.S.C. § 3730(h), and A.R.S. § 23-1501).
- **Investigative process:** the Executive Director assigns an investigator (normally the Quality
  & Compliance Coordinator), who secures records, interviews witnesses, documents findings, and
  recommends corrective action within 30 days; findings involving payments trigger repayment and
  self-disclosure as required.

## 4. False Claims Act and related laws

Staff are trained on the following, which apply to all federally funded programs:

- **False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733):** it is a violation to knowingly submit a false or
  fraudulent claim for payment; to make or use a false record or statement to conceal, avoid, or
  decrease an obligation to pay or transmit money or property to the Federal Government; or to
  conspire with others to have a false or fraudulent claim paid by the Federal Government. The FCA
  covers fraud involving any federally funded contract or program, with the exception of tax
  fraud. Remedies include treble damages and per-claim civil penalties; qui tam whistleblowers may
  share in recoveries and are protected from retaliation.
- **Deficit Reduction Act of 2005:** requires entities receiving significant Medicaid funds to
  educate employees about the FCA, state false claims laws, whistleblower protections, and the
  entity's own FWA policies — this policy and its training satisfy that requirement.
- **Stark Law (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn):** prohibits physician self-referral for designated health
  services where a financial relationship exists.
- **Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b):** prohibits knowingly offering, paying,
  soliciting, or receiving anything of value to induce or reward referrals of federal health care
  program business.
- **Arizona law:** A.R.S. § 36-2918 (AHCCCS false claims civil penalties) and A.R.S. § 13-2310
  (fraudulent schemes, a class 2 felony) provide state criminal and civil penalties for false
  claims and statements.

## 5. Training requirements

FWA/FCA training is completed at hire and annually for all staff (delivered by the Training
Coordinator, in person or online), covering this policy, the Code of Conduct, the laws above, how
to report, and whistleblower protections. Completion and comprehension are documented.

## 6. Monitoring and oversight

The Quality & Compliance Coordinator owns the FWA monitoring calendar (exclusion checks monthly;
billing reconciliation monthly; documentation audits quarterly) and reports results to the
Executive Director. The Program Integrity Requirements Verification (DDD-2090A) is maintained
current.

## 7. Compliance and corrective action

Substantiated FWA results in disciplinary action up to termination, repayment, disclosure to the
Division/AHCCCS, and referral for prosecution where warranted. Control weaknesses identified are
corrected through written CAPs tracked to completion.
