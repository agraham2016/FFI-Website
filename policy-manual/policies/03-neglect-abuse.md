## 1. Purpose

To define, prevent, detect, and respond to neglect, abuse, and exploitation of members,
consistent with DDD Provider Policy Manual Chapters 20 and 64, Operations Policy Manual Chapters
2000, 6000, and 6002-G, A.A.C. R6-6-1600, R6-6-808.I, R6-6-809.C.2 & D, R6-6-906.B, and
A.R.S. §§ 36-551.01, 36-569, 13-3620, and 46-454.

## 2. Definitions

- **Abuse:** intentional infliction of physical, emotional, or sexual harm — including hitting,
  rough handling, intimidation, humiliation, verbal assault, or any sexual contact with a member.
- **Neglect:** failure to provide the supervision, food, medical care, or services a member needs,
  resulting in or risking harm.
- **Exploitation:** use of a member or a member's resources (money, property, labor, image) for
  another person's benefit.

## 3. Policy

- FFI has zero tolerance for abuse, neglect, or exploitation by anyone — staff, family, community
  members, or other members.
- **All FFI staff are mandatory reporters** under A.R.S. § 13-3620 (children) and § 46-454
  (vulnerable adults). No supervisor approval is needed — or permitted to be required — before a
  staff member makes a mandated report.
- The DES/DDD-approved sign, **"Everyone Has the Right to be Safe,"** is posted at every service
  setting's telephone location and/or next to posted emergency numbers.
- The following behavioral intervention techniques are **prohibited** in all FFI settings: locked
  time-out rooms; overcorrection; application of noxious stimuli; and physical, mechanical, or
  pharmacologic restraints used as a negative consequence to behavior.
- Misuse or abuse of medications (routine, PRN, or one-time — including using medication to
  sedate or punish) is abuse and is reported and investigated as such.

## 4. Recognizing abuse and neglect

Staff are trained to recognize indicators, including: unexplained bruises, burns, or injuries;
fear or withdrawal around specific people; sudden behavior changes; poor hygiene or weight loss;
missing funds or property; isolation; and inconsistent explanations for injuries. Detection duty
extends to suspected abuse **occurring outside FFI services** (home, community, other providers):
staff report what they observe regardless of where it may have occurred.

## 5. Response procedures

1. **Protect first.** Immediately separate the member from the suspected source of harm, render
   aid, and call 911 if there is injury or danger.
2. **Report.** Verbally report immediately to APS (1-877-SOS-ADULT) for adults or DCS
   (1-888-SOS-CHILD) for children, law enforcement and/or Tribal Protective Services as
   applicable, and the Division; notify the On-Call Supervisor. Complete DDD-0191A within 24
   hours (see FFI-02).
3. **Remove implicated staff.** Any staff member alleged to have abused, neglected, or exploited a
   member is removed from member contact immediately pending investigation outcome.
4. **Immediate interventions.** The Program Manager/Employment Services Manager puts protective
   measures in place the same day (staffing changes, increased supervision, environmental fixes) to
   prevent further harm.
5. **Review and corrective action.** The Quality & Compliance Coordinator completes fact-finding,
   cooperates with external investigations, and develops a written corrective action plan with
   owners and due dates; the Executive Director approves and monitors it to completion.

## 6. Intervention techniques and treatment considerations

Support strategies must follow the member's Planning Document and Behavior Treatment Plan
(FFI-15). When interventions, treatments, or medications carry risks or side effects that may
adversely affect a member, those risks are documented, disclosed to the member/responsible person,
and monitored by the planning team.

## 7. Member training

FFI reviews personal-safety information with members at intake and at least annually, in plain
language and the member's preferred communication mode: what abuse/neglect/exploitation are, that
they have the right to be safe, and how to tell someone (including FFI's number, APS, and the
"Everyone Has the Right to be Safe" poster).

## 8. Staff training

Abuse/neglect/exploitation prevention training (including mandatory reporting, recognition,
prohibited techniques, and this policy) is completed at hire before member contact and annually
thereafter, delivered by the Training Coordinator; competency is verified and documented with
signed acknowledgment.

## 9. Monitoring, compliance, and corrective action

The Quality & Compliance Coordinator tracks all allegations, reports, investigation outcomes, and
corrective action plans, and reviews trends quarterly with the Executive Director (fed into
FFI-06). Violations of this policy result in disciplinary action up to termination, mandatory
referral to authorities, and system-level corrective action plans (CAPs) for any process failure.
