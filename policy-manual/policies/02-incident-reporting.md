## 1. Purpose

To ensure every incident affecting a member's health, safety, or rights is recognized, responded
to, documented, and reported consistent with DES/DDD policy (Provider Policy Manual Chapter 70;
Operations Policy Manual Chapters 2000, 6000, 6002; Behavior Supports Manual Chapter 900;
A.A.C. R6-6-803, R6-6-807, R6-6-809, R6-6-901).

## 2. What constitutes an incident

FFI uses the Division's definitions (Operations Manual 6002A) <mark>and AHCCCS Medical Policy Manual (AMPM) Policy 961</mark>. Examples include, but are not
limited to:

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| <mark>Sentinel events</mark> | Death; alleged abuse, neglect, or exploitation; missing member; suicide attempt; serious injury requiring medical treatment; law enforcement involvement; medication errors with harm; emergency measures/physical management |
| <mark>Reportable incidents</mark> | Minor injury; medication errors without harm; property damage; behavioral incidents without harm; service delivery disruptions |

Member health and safety always comes first: staff stabilize the situation, render aid, and call
911 when needed **before** any documentation.

## 3. Emergency roles and responsibilities

- **Medical emergency (e.g., choking):** the DSP on duty immediately performs trained first
  aid/CPR/abdominal thrusts as applicable, calls 911, does not leave the member alone, and
  notifies the On-Call Supervisor once the member is safe.
- **Fire or flood:** the DSP evacuates the member(s) to the designated safe location, calls 911,
  and follows the site evacuation plan (FFI-26).
- Emergency numbers, after-hours contacts for FFI management (On-Call Supervisor:
  (602) 663-1664), and emergency responder numbers are posted at each service site and carried in
  each service vehicle.

## 4. Reporting procedures and timeframes

1. **Immediate verbal report.** For <mark>Sentinel events</mark>, staff verbally notify the On-Call
   Supervisor immediately, and the Executive Director notifies the Division immediately (using the
   Division's after-hours line, (602) 375-1403 or 1-855-375-1403, outside business hours).
   Allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation additionally trigger mandatory reports to APS
   (1-877-767-2385), DCS (1-888-767-2445), and/or law enforcement or Tribal Social Services as
   applicable, before the end of the shift.
2. **<mark>Entry into the AHCCCS QM Portal.</mark>** <mark>All reportable incidents are entered directly into the
   [AHCCCS Quality Management (QM) Portal](https://qmportal.azahcccs.gov/) as an Incident,
   Accident, and Death (IAD) report, following the Division's AHCCCS QM Portal Migration User
   Guide and FAQs and the Provider Quick Guide for Incident Reporting.</mark>
   - <mark>**Sentinel events:** reported to the Division immediately, and entered into the QM Portal
     within one business day of the event.</mark>
   - <mark>**Reportable incidents:** entered into the QM Portal within two business days of the
     incident or of FFI being notified of it.</mark>
3. **<mark>Minimum required information.</mark>** <mark>Every QM Portal entry includes, at a minimum, the
   information required by the portal's IAD steps:</mark>
   - <mark>the member (name, date of birth, AHCCCS ID) and member information;</mark>
   - <mark>provider information (FFI, the service site, and the assigned Clinical Director);</mark>
   - <mark>basic incident information: date, time, location, and incident category/subcategory;</mark>
   - <mark>an objective, chronological description of the incident;</mark>
   - <mark>the member's condition before and after the incident, and any medical services received;</mark>
   - <mark>witnesses, including staff present;</mark>
   - <mark>provider actions taken and actions recommended to prevent recurrence;</mark>
   - <mark>notifications made (guardian/responsible person, Support Coordinator, APS/DCS, law
     enforcement), with names, dates, and times; and</mark>
   - <mark>supporting attachments, when relevant (for example, staff statements).</mark>
4. **Notifications.** The Program Manager or Employment Services Manager notifies the member's
   guardian/responsible person, DDD Support Coordinator, and (when involved in the member's care)
   Behavioral Health providers, the same day for Sentinel events and within one business day
   otherwise, and documents who was notified and when.
5. **<mark>QM Portal user roles.</mark>** <mark>The Executive Director is FFI's designated point of contact for all
   QM and incident reporting inquiries and holds FFI's QM Portal account. Responsibilities follow
   the portal's user roles:</mark>
   - <mark>**Preparer** — creates the IAD report from the staff member's account of the incident and
     e-signs it. The Executive Director is the Preparer until additional staff are granted the
     role; when they are, the Program Manager or Employment Services Manager prepares reports for
     their programs.</mark>
   - <mark>**Clinical Director** — reviews each report for accuracy and completeness, then approves it
     or returns it to the Preparer for correction. The Executive Director holds this role; when
     another person holds the Preparer role, the Clinical Director is never the same person who
     prepared the report.</mark>
   - <mark>FFI registers its QM Portal account within 30 days of becoming an AHCCCS-registered
     provider. Until the account is active, the Executive Director contacts Provider Network Support
     (providernetworksupport@azdes.gov) and reports incidents using the Division's current
     instructions, so that no reporting deadline is missed.</mark>
6. **Administrative review <mark>and event status</mark>.** Every report is reviewed for accuracy and
   completeness before final submission. Any revision is verified by the staff member who wrote the
   original account, and the original account is attached to the final report sent to the Division.
   <mark>The Preparer and Clinical Director track each report through its QM Portal status until it
   is submitted to the Division:</mark>
   - <mark>**Draft** — the Preparer saves and completes all required steps, then confirms the Report
     Validation step shows no missing information;</mark>
   - <mark>**Signed by the Preparer** — the Preparer e-signs, which sends the report to the assigned
     Clinical Director;</mark>
   - <mark>**Returned** — the Clinical Director returns a report that needs correction; the Preparer
     corrects it and e-signs again the same business day;</mark>
   - <mark>**Approved** — the Clinical Director approves and e-signs the report, which submits it to
     the Division for review; the Executive Director confirms the submission, and responds to any
     Division follow-up questions within the timeframe requested; and</mark>
   - <mark>**Withdrawn** — used only when the Clinical Director determines an entry is not reportable
     or duplicates a report already submitted, with the reason documented.</mark>

   <mark>The Executive Director checks open QM Portal reports every business day so that no report
   remains in Draft or Returned status past the deadlines above.</mark>

## 5. Incidents involving staff

If an alleged incident involves paid staff — including any allegation of abuse, neglect, or
exploitation — the staff member is immediately removed from work with or contact with members
pending the outcome of the investigation/fact-finding. Reassignment or leave is determined by the
Executive Director.

## 6. Fact-finding

The Quality & Compliance Coordinator (or Executive Director when the Coordinator is involved)
conducts internal fact-finding: securing the scene/records, interviewing staff and witnesses,
reviewing documentation, and producing a written summary with corrective actions. FFI cooperates
fully with Division, APS, DCS, and law enforcement investigations.

## 7. Training requirements

All staff complete incident reporting and mandatory reporting training at hire (before working
with members) and annually, delivered by the Training Coordinator (in person or online), including
<mark>how incidents are entered in the AHCCCS QM Portal, differentiating Sentinel events from reportable
incidents,</mark> and the Provider Quick Guide for Incident Reporting. Staff sign an acknowledgment that they understand they are mandatory
reporters. This policy is reviewed with all direct care staff at least annually and whenever
revised, with signed verification.

## 8. Documentation, monitoring, and oversight

- All incident reports, fact-finding records, and notification logs are retained per FFI-05.
- The Quality & Compliance Coordinator logs every incident in a tracking system (date, member,
  type, severity, notifications, corrective actions, closure date) and reviews trends quarterly
  with the Executive Director; trends feed the Quality Management Plan (FFI-06).

## 9. Compliance and corrective action

Failure to report, late reporting, or falsification results in disciplinary action up to
termination and any legally required referral. Systemic gaps identified through trending result in
corrective action plans with assigned owners and completion dates.
