## 1. Purpose

To ensure member health, safety, and service continuity through any emergency — staffing loss,
facility loss, utility/system failure, natural disaster, or pandemic — per the QVA (Contingency
Plan, I; Standard Terms and Conditions) and DDD Provider Policy Manual Chapter 36 (Fire Safety).

## 2. Health and safety in an emergency

The plan's first priority is the member: staff on duty secure member safety (evacuate, shelter,
render aid, call 911 as needed), then notify the On-Call Supervisor at (602) 663-1664, who
activates this plan and required notifications (FFI-02, FFI-12).

## 3. Workforce contingency

- **Key succession and performance planning:** if there is a sudden significant decrease in FFI's
  workforce (including loss of a key leader), pre-designated successors act in each role —
  Executive Director duties fall to the Program Manager; Program Manager duties to the Employment
  Services Manager (and vice versa) — until permanent coverage is in place.
- **Alternative service methods:** service continuity options include redeploying administrative
  staff with current DSP qualifications, adjusting schedules to cover plan-critical supports
  first, cross-agency staffing agreements, and (where appropriate and authorized) remote support
  check-ins.
- **Contact list and org chart:** FFI maintains an up-to-date contact list of all Qualified Vendor
  contacts by title, with an organizational chart, reviewed quarterly.
- **Staff call chart (phone tree):** a phone tree is maintained for emergencies; the On-Call
  Supervisor initiates it; each branch confirms completion back up the tree.

## 4. Facility and systems contingencies

- **Loss of facilities/sites:** if the FFI office or a service site becomes unusable, operations
  relocate to a pre-identified alternate location; members' services continue in their homes and
  communities wherever possible.
- **Electronic/telephone failure at the primary place of business:** calls forward to the On-Call
  cell number; leadership uses a group messaging channel as backup; the published number
  (602) 663-1664 remains reachable.
- **Loss of computer systems/records:** electronic records are backed up (FFI-05 §5); restoration
  is verified after any outage; paper fallback forms are staged for documentation continuity.
- **Facility evacuation plan:** each site maintains a posted evacuation route and assembly point;
  drills are conducted and documented; staff are trained at site orientation (fire safety per
  Provider Manual Ch. 36).
- **Shelter-in-place plan:** each supported-living site maintains supplies to shelter in place
  with adequate staffing levels, food, water, prescribed medications, and equipment meeting
  members' needs for **not less than 3 days** and for the duration of the emergency/disaster;
  supplies are inventoried quarterly.

## 5. Pandemic plan

During a declared pandemic or infectious disease emergency, FFI will:

1. Follow ADHS/CDC and Division guidance (including any DDD Qualified Vendor pandemic FAQs);
2. Implement infection control: screening, PPE per guidance, hygiene protocols, and enhanced
   cleaning at all sites;
3. Prioritize continuity of plan-critical services; document and report any service gaps to the
   Division;
4. Use the workforce contingency measures in §3 for staffing shortages, with member-preference and
   health-risk-based prioritization;
5. Communicate status changes to members, families, and the Division promptly (FFI-12); and
6. Track staff/member exposures and cases, reporting as required by public health authorities and
   the Division.

## 6. Training, monitoring, compliance

All staff are trained on this plan at hire and annually; site-specific evacuation/shelter training
occurs at site orientation. The Executive Director tests the phone tree and reviews the full plan
annually and after every activation (lessons-learned review feeding FFI-06). Failures to follow
the plan are reviewed for retraining, discipline, and plan revision.
