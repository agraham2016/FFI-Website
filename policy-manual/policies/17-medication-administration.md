## 1. Purpose

To define whether and how FFI staff handle member medications in supported living settings, per
A.A.C. R6-6-806.C, R6-6-807.A, R6-6-808.G & I, R6-6-809.C, R6-6-902.B, R6-6-903.A, R6-6-908.B,
A.R.S. § 36-551.01.O and § 36-557.O, DDD Operations Policy Manual Chapter 6002, and DDD Medical
Policy Manual Chapter 310-V.

## 2. Policy: who may administer

- FFI DSPs who have completed the Division-recognized Medication Administration training (and
  member-specific orientation) **may assist with and administer medications** to supported-living
  members as identified in the member's Planning Document. Staff without current training may not
  handle medications.
- FFI verifies each member's PCSP/Planning Document to confirm the member's access to their
  medications and the level of support required (independent self-administration, reminders,
  assistance, or full administration). In any licensed setting, all members have the same access —
  medications are either locked or unlocked consistently per setting policy and member plans.

## 3. Requirements when staff administer medications

1. **Training:** employee medication administration training is documented with completion and
   expiration dates; refreshers occur before expiration.
2. **Storage:** medications are stored securely in original labeled containers, locked when
   required by the setting/plan, separated per member, with proper temperature control.
3. **Error reporting:** medication errors (wrong member, drug, dose, route, time, or missed dose)
   are reported immediately to the supervisor, the family/guardian, and DDD (incident report per
   FFI-02), with medical advice sought as needed.
4. **Medication log:** a Medication Administration Record (MAR) tracks every administration —
   medication, dose, route, time, initials.
5. **Disposal:** expired or discontinued medications are documented and disposed of per
   pharmacy/DEA guidance (witnessed, logged).
6. **PRN and behavior-modifying medications:** "as needed" administrations are documented with
   reason, time, and outcome; behavior-modifying medications follow FFI-15 §4.

## 4. Physician orders

- **All medication requires written physician orders — including over-the-counter medication.**
- A verbal order from a medical practitioner must be followed by a written order **within 72
  hours**.

## 5. Administration procedure (step-by-step)

1. Wash hands; prepare a clean surface (prevention of contamination).
2. Verify the **right member, right medication, right dose, right time, right route** against the
   MAR and label.
3. Administer per route-specific training (oral, topical, rectal, or other trained routes).
4. Document immediately on the MAR; never pre-sign.
5. Observe for and report adverse reactions.
6. **Errors:** report per §3.3; the Quality & Compliance Coordinator reviews, tracks, and trends
   all errors and drives corrective actions.
7. **Refills:** staff monitor supply, request refills at least 7 days before exhaustion, and
   maintain an adequate supply.
8. **Self-administration:** members self-administer when the Planning Document team approves in
   writing; criteria for self-administration and documentation requirements are recorded in the
   plan, and staff document required supports.

## 6. Monitoring, compliance, and corrective action

Supervisors audit MARs weekly; the Quality & Compliance Coordinator trends errors monthly.
Untrained staff handling medications, falsified MARs, or repeated errors result in immediate
removal from medication duties, retraining, and progressive discipline.

## 7. Forms and references

**Forms:** MAR; medication error report (with DDD-0191A when reportable); medication disposal log;
physician order file.
