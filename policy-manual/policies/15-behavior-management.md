## 1. Purpose

To ensure behavior support is positive, person-centered, and compliant with Article 9
(A.A.C. R6-6-900 et seq.), the DDD Behavior Supports Manual (including Chapter 900 — Emergency
Measures and Physical Management Techniques), Provider Policy Manual Chapter 66, and
A.A.C. R6-6-1007, R6-6-1107, R6-6-808.G & I, R6-6-809.B.

## 2. Policy

- **Prevention first.** Staff are trained in prevention and positive behavior support as
  applicable to their assignments: recognizing cues, reinforcing appropriate behavior,
  redirection, environmental evaluation, consistency, clear communication, and offering choices.
- **Article 9 alignment.** All behavior-related practices, plans, and training follow Article 9
  requirements; techniques prohibited by Article 9 and FFI-03 §3 are never used.
- <mark>**P.R.O.U.D. training.** Staff who will support a member with a behavior plan or documented
  unsafe behavior complete P.R.O.U.D. (Providing Respectful Opportunities and Understanding
  De-escalation), taught by a Division-certified P.R.O.U.D. instructor, before working with that
  member. All DSPs hired after July 31, 2026, and any DSP retrained in member intervention
  techniques as a corrective action, are trained in P.R.O.U.D.; staff holding current Prevention &
  Support certification complete P.R.O.U.D. by December 31, 2027. Certification is renewed on the
  schedule set by the Division and tracked by the Training Coordinator (FFI-08).</mark>
- **Emergency measures.** Any use of emergency measures is an incident: staff follow Behavior
  Supports Manual Chapter 900, ensure member safety, and report per FFI-02 (immediate verbal
  report; <mark>entry into the AHCCCS QM Portal within the FFI-02 timeframes</mark>).

## <mark>3. Prohibited and restricted techniques</mark>

<mark>**Prohibited techniques** (A.A.C. R6-6-902; Behavior Supports Manual Chapter 200) are never used
with any member, under any circumstances:</mark>

- <mark>seclusion (locked time-out rooms, or holding a door shut to prevent a member from leaving);</mark>
- <mark>overcorrection;</mark>
- <mark>noxious stimuli (offensive tastes, sounds, odors, touch, or sights);</mark>
- <mark>physical or mechanical restraint used as a consequence for behavior, and any lying (supine,
  prone, or side) restraint or floor-seated restraint that folds or presses the body forward;</mark>
- <mark>behavior-modifying medication given "as needed" (PRN), in doses that interfere with daily
  living, or without a behavior plan, except as allowed by R6-6-909; and</mark>
- <mark>any treatment prohibited by A.R.S. § 36-561(A).</mark>

<mark>**Techniques that require Program Review Committee (PRC) approval** (A.A.C. R6-6-903; Behavior
Supports Manual Chapter 400) are used only after the behavior plan containing them has been
approved by the PRC and reviewed by the Independent Oversight Committee (IOC), and the responsible
person has consented:</mark>

- <mark>techniques that require the use of force (for example, forced compliance or forced exclusion
  time-out);</mark>
- <mark>response-cost programs;</mark>
- <mark>programs that might infringe on the member's rights (for example, exclusion from routine
  activities, restitution, earning basic necessities, or limits on community access or
  possessions);</mark>
- <mark>behavior-modifying medications (except in the member's own or family home); and</mark>
- <mark>protective devices used to prevent self-injury.</mark>

## <mark>4. Behavior plan writer</mark>

<mark>The Program Manager is FFI's designated behavior plan coordinator. When a member's planning team
determines a behavior plan is needed, the plan is written by a qualified behavior plan writer who
meets the Behavior Supports Manual Chapter 800 requirements, either a professional contracted by
FFI or the writer assigned through the member's planning team, and is written on the Division's
Behavior Plan template. The writer develops the plan by:</mark>

1. <mark>observing the member in multiple environments, at different times, and during different
   activities, following the member's lead;</mark>
2. <mark>interviewing the people who spend the most time with the member, using formal or informal
   assessment tools;</mark>
3. <mark>reviewing the member's history, antecedent-behavior-consequence (ABC) data, and incident
   reports related to behavior;</mark>
4. <mark>defining the target behaviors, identifying their function, and identifying replacement
   behaviors and the positive strategies to teach them;</mark>
5. <mark>naming who will train implementing staff and who will monitor the plan; and</mark>
6. <mark>reviewing the draft with the planning team and the responsible person for consent before PRC
   submission.</mark>

## 5. Behavior Treatment Plans (BTPs)

1. **Timely PRC submission.** When a member needs a BTP that requires review (techniques requiring
   force, response cost, potential rights infringement, behavior-modifying medications, or
   protective devices), FFI participates with the planning team so the plan is submitted to the
   Program Review Committee (PRC) — and IOC where applicable — **within 90 days of placement or of
   the need being identified**. The Program Manager tracks submission dates.
2. **Implementation.** Assigned staff are trained on each member's BTP before implementing it;
   implementation is documented every shift.
3. **Effectiveness monitoring.** Behavior data is collected as specified in the BTP, graphed or
   summarized monthly, and reviewed by the Program Manager with the planning team; ineffective
   plans are returned to the team for revision.
4. <mark>**Tracking system (Behavior Supports Manual Chapter 800).** The Program Manager maintains a
   behavior plan tracking log for every member with a plan, recording the target behaviors and
   their monthly data, the PRC submission and approval dates, the plan expiration date, and the
   date of each monitoring observation. The plan monitor named by the planning team (never a staff
   member who implements the plan) observes implementation on site at least twice a month and
   documents whether the plan is implemented accurately and consistently, whether data collection
   is complete and accurate, the member's progress toward goals, and any barriers or needed
   revisions. Data is evaluated at least monthly to judge effectiveness; plans expiring within 60
   days are flagged for renewal.</mark>

## 6. Behavior-modifying medications

For members using behavior-modifying medications, FFI's procedures include:

- **Quarterly medication reviews** documented with the components listed under
  A.A.C. R6-6-806.M (medication, dosage, purpose, effectiveness, side effects, and review
  outcome), coordinated with the prescriber and planning team;
- **Screening for Tardive Dyskinesia** on the schedule set by the prescriber/planning team, with
  results documented; and
- **Prohibitions on psychotropic medication misuse** — never used as punishment, for staff
  convenience, or as a substitute for services, and only with informed consent and PRC review as
  required.

## 7. Documentation of maladaptive behavior

Staff document maladaptive behavior objectively (antecedent–behavior–consequence, duration,
intensity, intervention used, outcome) in the member record the same shift; patterns are raised to
the planning team.

## 8. Training and verification

- DSPs complete positive behavior support training at hire and annually; <mark>P.R.O.U.D. (certified
  instructor) as described in §2</mark> (FFI-08).
- Direct Care Workers' training on this policy's requirements is documented with signed, dated
  records.
- This policy is reviewed by all DCWs at hire, at least annually, and whenever revised, with
  signed verification tracked by the Training Coordinator.

## 9. Monitoring, compliance, and corrective action

The Program Manager reviews behavior documentation monthly; the Quality & Compliance Coordinator
audits BTP submission timeliness, quarterly medication review documentation, and training
verification quarterly. Use of a prohibited technique triggers immediate removal from member
contact, incident reporting, investigation, and discipline up to termination.
